The whole deal

facing-dani

A small marketing site for a Calgary nail, lash and makeup artist. This page is the project's status board. It answers where things stand in a few seconds, without reading a transcript. /overview writes this view and the roadmap from the two plan docs; every feature adds its own group as it starts.

From the project plan

1 · Problem
Instagram shows her work but cannot hold a price list, cannot be found on Google, and is a poor gallery when someone wants to inspect one set closely. The site is what her bio link points at, and the job stays narrow: look like a real business, show the work at a size where quality is visible, answer what/how much/how long without a DM, and take the booking. What she sells is craft, not price - trained in Colombia, and taught to get right the shape and cuticle work that decides whether a set still looks good in week three. Price is deliberately not the angle, because it is the one advantage that disappears the moment she raises her rates.
2 · Users
Four, and the first one decides nearly everything. The client, roughly 15 to 25, arrives from an Instagram bio link, on a phone, at night, already choosing between artists she follows. Her mother, paying for a grad set, needs the business to look legitimate in four seconds and will not DM a teenager, which is why the form exists. The Spanish-speaking client searches in Spanish and currently finds nothing local. Dani herself needs one link so her DMs stop being a price list.
3 · Features
Seven pages in two languages: home, a gallery, one page per service, contact and a thank-you page. Her story and the trust a new business has not earned yet live on the home page, not a separate About page. Three rules decide what is in: both languages per page (a page is not done in one), SEO ships with the page it describes, and no placeholder text - Spanish written by Dani, not by a translator. Out: payment, deposits, real-time availability, client accounts, on-site reviews, a blog.
4 · Data
Nothing in a database, and that is a decision. Every fact is a typed file in the repo, so it is version controlled and reviewable in a diff. The split that matters: prices, durations, ImageKit paths and ordering are shared; names, descriptions, alt text, FAQ copy and metadata are locale-keyed. A price stored twice will one day disagree with itself. Images are records, never files. The only thing that leaves the machine is a contact form submission, emailed and not retained.
5 · Tech
Next.js 16 and React 19, App Router, no separate backend. shadcn on Base UI with lucide, Tailwind v4 with the theme as CSS variables in @theme, react-hook-form with zod validated on both sides, Resend from a Server Action, ImageKit doing real work for a photo-led gallery. i18n is hand-rolled - a [locale] segment and a dictionary per locale, no package. No database, no auth, no object storage, no motion library; all four were offered by the scaffolder and declined.
6 · Monetize
The site does not make money. It is a sales asset, and the money is made in a chair. Success is bookings traceable to the site, measured by a "how did you find me" field plus Search Console - no analytics script, no cookie banner. The real return is the template. Calgary has many beauty businesses, most Latin-owned, and almost none have a bilingual site. That discipline costs nothing on client one and is worth a great deal on client two.
7 · UI/UX
After Party. Vibrant and active, never spa-calm. Plum ground #261F36, four colour-coded accents, Unbounded over Inter, mobile first because the site is an Instagram bio link. Until she has photos for them, lashes and makeup carry licensed decorative stock with no identifiable faces - never in the gallery or a work strip, never captioned as hers. Every service page, nails included, also carries a stock grid headed "Inspiration" in both languages, which stays when her photos arrive because it shows styles, not her work. Decided from rendered options in blueprint/reference/, not from adjectives. Written in full in project-plan.md.
8 · Deployment
Live on Vercel at facing-dani.vercel.app, building from main, with a preview per pushed branch. ImageKit is configured in production and the gallery serves from it; Resend sends as glammedbeautystudio.com since Sep 24, verified; production picks it up when item 15 ships. English at the root and Spanish at /es, not a subdomain, because that splits a ranking signal this site has none of to spare. Deploy early and keep deploying, so Dani watches it grow on her phone and corrections arrive while they are cheap. The domain is glammedbeautystudio.com, bought Sep 24 and live on Vercel. Indexing stays closed until item 15 ships with the rename to Glammed Beauty Studio, so Google's first look carries the right name. A Google Business Profile will out-earn the site for the first year and is part of the engagement, not an afterthought.

Locked decisions

  • The ground is #261F36, a plum rather than a black. Picked over darker options that had more neon punch but read as severe.
  • Four accents, each with a job: pink is nails, violet is lashes, orange is makeup, mint is confirmation. They are navigation, never decoration.
  • Every accent needs a darker twin. The fills fail contrast under 18px, so small text and prices use the darker value, never the fill.
  • Type is Unbounded 800 for headlines and Inter for everything else. Anton read as a gym poster, Fredoka as a cereal box.
  • Dark is the brand. A light twin exists, and the rule is that glow becomes tint. The gradient CTA and ticker are identical in both, which is what keeps it one brand.
  • Whether visitors get a theme toggle is still open. Leaning no.
  • Mobile is the product, not a consideration. Every mockup was drawn at phone width.
  • Her photos need dark-friendly backgrounds. She has to hear this before she shoots, not after.
  • Motion in CSS only. motion is not installed and is not being added.
  • A cast is not a check. as keyof T asserts that a string is a key of T; it does not verify it. One of those silenced the error that would have caught three empty footer links before they shipped. Any as inside a lookup marks the spot where the compiler was asked to stop helping.
  • SEO is the slow second engine here. Google Business Profile and her Instagram will out-earn the site for the first year.
  • Spanish is not an SEO target. English is what the site is trying to rank. The Spanish pages exist so someone who reads Spanish more comfortably can use the site, and hreflang is there to stop Google reading the two as duplicates and dropping one - which protects the English ranking. No Spanish keyword work. Decided 2026-09-21, replacing an earlier line in the plan that treated Spanish as a second set of rankings.
  • At 17 and likely working from home, a street address in the schema is a privacy call, not a default.
  • No component contains the word Dani. A component reads siteConfig.business.name. The test is whether a second beauty business could be stood up by editing data/ and the theme and nothing else. If the answer is no, that is a bug and not a preference.
  • A price lives in exactly one place. Prices, durations, ImageKit paths and ordering are shared across locales; only prose is keyed by locale. Storing a price twice guarantees they disagree one day, and the visitor will believe whichever one is wrong.
  • A missing Spanish string is a build error, never a silent fallback to English. Falling back quietly is how a site ends up half-translated without anyone noticing.
  • Both languages and its own SEO are the definition of done, not a finishing pass. A page that exists in one language is not done, and its metadata, JSON-LD and hreflang land in the same commit as the page.
  • The gallery comes before the pages that describe the work, because it is the page that sells it.

Locale routing

The [locale] segment, a dictionary per locale, the language switch, and the build error on a missing string. Hand-rolled, no package: two languages and eight pages do not need next-intl. Second because retrofitting a second language means reopening every page.

Spec agreed Built & verified Commit granted
What was built 4 steps · both locales prerendered
URLs
English at /, Spanish at /es, both prerendered. /en redirects away, so there is never a second URL for the same page.
Dictionaries
English is the shape source and Spanish is annotated with it, so a missing key is a type error and the build fails. No fallback, by design.
Shell
Nav, CTA, footer, menu and skip link all translated. Every link locale-aware through one helper. hreflang set once on the locale layout.
Switch
Swaps language on the current page rather than returning home. A real link, so it works with JavaScript off.
Decisions 3, with what the rejected option cost
  • English is deliberately not as const. That looked like the obvious way to type the dictionary, but it makes every value its own string literal, so Spanish would have to repeat the English words exactly to typecheck. It would have enforced sameness instead of completeness, which is the opposite of the point.
  • The rewrites are written one per route, not as a catch-all. A catch-all with a negative lookahead runs before the filesystem and has to exclude _next and static files by regex. That is the rule that looks right and silently swallows an asset. An explicit list cannot.
  • The dictionary reaches the client components as a prop, never an import. Importing it there would pull both languages into the bundle, so every English visitor would download the Spanish strings.
Still open 2, neither blocking
  • The Spanish copy is a draft and needs Dani. Every Spanish string in the shell was written by the build, not by a native speaker. "Trained in Colombia" especially is a sales line rather than a sentence to translate literally.
  • Spanish slugs are still mirrored English (/es/nails, not /es/unas). One helper owns the prefix, so deciding is a one-file change.

How her email works the diagram, the pieces, the records
Vercel DNS
Holds every record. Reached through the project's Domains tab, then View DNS Records; the domain does not appear in the team-level Domains list.
ImprovMX
Receiving only. Account on her Gmail, catch-all to it. Records: MX mx1.improvmx.com 10, MX mx2.improvmx.com 20, TXT v=spf1 include:spf.improvmx.com ~all, all on @, added by hand.
Resend
Sending only, receiving off. Its records sit on the send subdomain and resend._domainkey, added by Resend's automatic setup, so its SPF never collides with ImprovMX's.
Her Gmail
danisglammedstudio@gmail.com. Send mail as dani@ through smtp.resend.com on 587 with TLS, username resend, password a sending-only key named "Gmail - Dani". dani@ is the default, and replies always come from it.

What bit 4, each would have lost mail quietly
  • Two of three form enquiries bounced. ImprovMX refuses mail from IPs on the SpamCop list, and Resend sends from shared Amazon SES IPs that land on it now and then. The visitor still saw "Got it, thank you". The form now delivers straight to her Gmail, which does not use SpamCop; mail customers send from their own inboxes is unaffected.
  • "Reply from the same address" would have leaked her Gmail. Form enquiries arrive at the Gmail address directly, so that setting replies from it. Gmail is set to always reply from the default, dani@.
  • ImprovMX looked finished and was not. Its screen showed the domain while Vercel held no MX records, so every message would have bounced. Only a lookup against ns1.vercel-dns.com showed it. Resend could write the records itself; ImprovMX could not.
  • Gmail hides "make default" until the address is verified, and pre-fills ImprovMX's server as the SMTP host. Both look like a setting that silently did nothing.
Decisions 4, with why the rejected option lost
  • The domain lives in code, not an env var. The variable was a lock for a site live without a domain. The rule, a null website that keeps Google out until the domain lands, is now in the nextjs blueprint standards.
  • Forwarding plus Gmail over Google Workspace. $0 against about $8 a month, and she keeps the one app she knows. Workspace wins the day she hires someone; dani@ would not change.
  • dani@ over info@. Personal for a solo teen artist. The catch-all means info@ or book@ already reach her if they are ever published.
  • One Resend key per job. Rotating the key named for Primo must never stop Dani's replies. Gmail has its own sending-only key; the site gets its own in 15.5.
