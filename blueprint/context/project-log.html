The whole deal

facing-dani

A small marketing site for a Calgary nail, lash and makeup artist. This page is the project's status board. It answers where things stand in a few seconds, without reading a transcript. /overview writes this view and the roadmap from the two plan docs; every feature adds its own group as it starts.

From the project plan

1 · Problem
Instagram shows her work but cannot hold a price list, cannot be found on Google, and is a poor gallery when someone wants to inspect one set closely. The site is what her bio link points at, and the job stays narrow: look like a real business, show the work at a size where quality is visible, answer what/how much/how long without a DM, and take the booking. What she sells is craft, not price - trained in Colombia, drilled on the shape and cuticle work that decides whether a set still looks good in week three. Price is deliberately not the angle, because it is the one advantage that disappears the moment she raises her rates.
2 · Users
Four, and the first one decides nearly everything. The client, roughly 15 to 25, arrives from an Instagram bio link, on a phone, at night, already choosing between artists she follows. Her mother, paying for a grad set, needs the business to look legitimate in four seconds and will not DM a teenager, which is why the form exists. The Spanish-speaking client searches in Spanish and currently finds nothing local. Dani herself needs one link so her DMs stop being a price list.
3 · Features
Eight pages in two languages: home, a gallery, one page per service, about, contact and a thank-you page. Three rules decide what is in: both languages per page (a page is not done in one), SEO ships with the page it describes, and no placeholder text - Spanish written by Dani, not by a translator. Out: payment, deposits, real-time availability, client accounts, on-site reviews, a blog.
4 · Data
Nothing in a database, and that is a decision. Every fact is a typed file in the repo, so it is version controlled and reviewable in a diff. The split that matters: prices, durations, ImageKit paths and ordering are shared; names, descriptions, alt text, FAQ copy and metadata are locale-keyed. A price stored twice will one day disagree with itself. Images are records, never files. The only thing that leaves the machine is a contact form submission, emailed and not retained.
5 · Tech
Next.js 16 and React 19, App Router, no separate backend. shadcn on Base UI with lucide, Tailwind v4 with the theme as CSS variables in @theme, react-hook-form with zod validated on both sides, Resend from a Server Action, ImageKit doing real work for a photo-led gallery. i18n is hand-rolled - a [locale] segment and a dictionary per locale, no package. No database, no auth, no object storage, no motion library; all four were offered by the scaffolder and declined.
6 · Monetize
The site does not make money. It is a sales asset, and the money is made in a chair. Success is bookings traceable to the site, measured by a "how did you find me" field plus Search Console - no analytics script, no cookie banner. The real return is the template. Calgary has many beauty businesses, most Latin-owned, and almost none have a bilingual site. That discipline costs nothing on client one and is worth a great deal on client two.
7 · UI/UX
After Party. Vibrant and active, never spa-calm. Plum ground #261F36, four colour-coded accents, Unbounded over Inter, mobile first because the site is an Instagram bio link. Decided from rendered options in blueprint/reference/, not from adjectives. Written in full in project-plan.md.
8 · Deployment
Vercel, npm run build, no custom start command, three env vars, no database and no workers. English at the root and Spanish at /es - not a subdomain, because that splits a ranking signal this site has none of to spare. Deploy early and keep deploying, so Dani watches it grow on her phone and corrections arrive while they are cheap. The domain is not registered yet. A Google Business Profile will out-earn the site for the first year and is part of the engagement, not an afterthought.

Locked decisions

  • The ground is #261F36, a plum rather than a black. Picked over darker options that had more neon punch but read as severe.
  • Four accents, each with a job: pink is nails, violet is lashes, orange is makeup, mint is confirmation. They are navigation, never decoration.
  • Every accent needs a darker twin. The fills fail contrast under 18px, so small text and prices use the darker value, never the fill.
  • Type is Unbounded 800 for headlines and Inter for everything else. Anton read as a gym poster, Fredoka as a cereal box.
  • Dark is the brand. A light twin exists, and the rule is that glow becomes tint. The gradient CTA and ticker are identical in both, which is what keeps it one brand.
  • Whether visitors get a theme toggle is still open. Leaning no.
  • Mobile is the product, not a consideration. Every mockup was drawn at phone width.
  • Her photos need dark-friendly backgrounds. She has to hear this before she shoots, not after.
  • Motion in CSS only. motion is not installed and is not being added.
  • SEO is the slow second engine here. Google Business Profile and her Instagram will out-earn the site for the first year.
  • At 17 and likely working from home, a street address in the schema is a privacy call, not a default.
  • No component contains the word Dani. A component reads siteConfig.business.name. The test is whether a second beauty business could be stood up by editing data/ and the theme and nothing else. If the answer is no, that is a bug and not a preference.
  • A price lives in exactly one place. Prices, durations, ImageKit paths and ordering are shared across locales; only prose is keyed by locale. Storing a price twice guarantees they disagree one day, and the visitor will believe whichever one is wrong.
  • A missing Spanish string is a build error, never a silent fallback to English. Falling back quietly is how a site ends up half-translated without anyone noticing.
  • Both languages and its own SEO are the definition of done, not a finishing pass. A page that exists in one language is not done, and its metadata, JSON-LD and hreflang land in the same commit as the page.
  • The gallery comes before the pages that describe the work, because it is the page that sells it.

Open questions

  • The light theme has no owner. Section 7 specifies the light twin in detail and there is a rendered sketch, but no build item builds it and the toggle is left "leaning no". Dark only, light via prefers-color-scheme inside item 1, or its own item - only one of those is free. Worth deciding before item 1.
  • Route slugs, and whether the Spanish ones are translated. /es/unas or /es/nails. Not cosmetic: it sets the hreflang pairing and the Spanish keyword targets. Decide at item 2.
  • Google Business Profile and Search Console are not in the build plan. Section 8 calls the profile more important than the site for the first year and part of the engagement, but nothing tracks it. Either it gets an item or it is explicitly outside the plan.
  • The business name is settled in one section and open in another. Section 4 names her Dani Moreno; section 8's domain row still reads "follows the business name, so danimoreno.ca or a studio name". The site and the profile must match character for character, so this has to be final first.
  • Whether service pages carry their own FAQs. The data shape allows it and section 8 wants each service page to own its terms, but only the home page surfaces FAQ. Cheap at item 5, expensive to retrofit into the structured data.
