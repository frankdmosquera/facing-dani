Same brand, two grounds

Dark is your step 3 plum, #261F36. Light is its twin. The accents, the type and the layout do not change. What changes is the mechanism: on dark the colour arrives as glow, on light it arrives as tint.

Home

The glow blobs behind the dark hero become flat tinted circles on light. Same position, same colours, no blur. Blur on a light ground just looks like a smudge.

The two rules that make it work

Glow becomes tint

  • Dark: blurred colour blobs behind everything, dark cards, the photo sits on a near-black panel.
  • Light: the same blobs unblurred and pale, white cards, each service card tinted its own colour.
  • The gradient CTA and the gradient ticker are identical in both. They are the one thing that never flips, which is what keeps it feeling like one brand.

Every accent needs a darker twin

  • The fills stay the same in both modes. The text versions do not.
  • Hot pink on white fails contrast below 18px, so small text and prices use the darker twin instead. Same for orange and violet.
  • Face and body hit this exact problem and solved it the same way. It is the one thing worth copying from that project.

One thing worth deciding before we build it. Do you want a visitor-facing toggle, or are you choosing one and committing? They are different jobs. A toggle means every section, every photo treatment and every colour gets checked twice, forever, and it roughly doubles the theme work.

My honest read: pick dark and commit. Brands do not flip. Nobody expects a nail artist's site to have a sun icon in the corner, her clients will never look for one, and the dark plum is the whole point of the direction. The light twin still earns its place as the mode for anything that has to print, and as proof the palette is not a one-trick.

If you do want the toggle, the scaffold already supports it: app/globals.css line 5 declares the dark variant and line 86 has the .dark block waiting. It is a real option, just not a free one.
